Teaching

Building capacity, together.

Methodological literacy is one of the most empowering things we can give the next generation of urban professionals. My teaching aims to make rigorous spatial thinking accessible, reproducible, and genuinely useful — for designers, planners, and researchers alike.

Philosophy

Data as a design tool.

The data revolution gave us new instruments — but more importantly, it gave us a new way of thinking. I teach from this premise: that learning to work with spatial data is not a technical skill, it is a cognitive shift that allows designers and planners to engage with complexity at a scale that was previously impossible.

Maps are everywhere now. Perhaps there has never been a moment when so many people could read and use a map — to navigate, to understand, to advocate. That is a profound cultural shift, and it carries a responsibility: to teach data literacy as a form of civic and creative power.

I also research and teach the role of AI in spatial design education — exploring how computational intelligence can become a design partner, and what new literacies students need to work with it critically and creatively.
