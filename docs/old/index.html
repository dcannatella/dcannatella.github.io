Research Interests & Approach

How I think about cities

My research sits at the intersection of urbanism, landscape architecture, and spatial data science — with urban resilience and climate adaptation as the central thread. I am drawn to questions about how cities respond to pressure, where they fail, and what kinds of spatial interventions actually make a measurable difference.

I work predominantly with quantitative methods — GIS, QGIS, R — applying spatial analysis and modeling to understand urban systems and assess the performance of design and planning solutions. What drives me is the belief that better questions and better tools can lead to better cities.

Lately I have been deeply curious about what artificial intelligence means for how we teach and practice spatial and urban design — not as a shortcut, but as a genuinely new way of engaging with complex spatial problems.

Teaching & Community

Building capacity, together

I believe that methodological literacy is one of the most empowering things we can give to the next generation of urban professionals. At TU Delft, I coordinate the Spatial Data and GIS learning line in the MSc Urbanism programme — making rigorous spatial thinking accessible, reproducible, and genuinely useful for designers and planners.

I am also a founding and core member of the Rbanism Community — an open initiative advancing the use of R in urbanism and spatial research. Building that community has reinforced something I care about deeply: sharing methods and knowledge across disciplines makes all of our work better.
